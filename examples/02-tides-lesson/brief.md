# Brief: Tides science lesson

**Input style:** a full slide-by-slide outline for a young audience.

- **Type:** science lesson for a Year 6 class (ages 10–12), about 15 minutes
- **Topic:** tides
- **Slides:** exactly 5
- **Tone:** simple, friendly language for children, with large text

## Slide 1: Cover
- "Why does the sea go in and out?"
- Year 6 Science · Earth and Space

## Slide 2: What is a tide?
- The sea slowly rises up the beach (high tide) and slides back down (low tide)
- Most beaches get two high tides and two low tides a day
- The pattern repeats about every 12 hours and 25 minutes

## Slide 3: The Moon pulls the ocean
- The Moon's gravity pulls on Earth's water
- The water bulges out on the side facing the Moon, and on the opposite side too
- As Earth spins, your beach moves through the bulges (high tide) and the gaps between them (low tide)
- Show this as a simple native diagram: Earth, the two bulges, the Moon and an arrow for Earth's spin

## Slide 4: Spring tides and neap tides
- Sun, Moon and Earth in a line (new moon, full moon): extra-high and extra-low tides, called spring tides
- Sun and Moon at a right angle (half moon): smaller tides, called neap tides
- Two small side-by-side diagrams
- Fun fact: "spring" here means "to leap up", not the season

## Slide 5: Be a tide detective
- Before rock-pooling, check the tide times for your beach
- Keep an eye on the water and on your way back to shore
- Always go with an adult
- Quick quiz: "If high tide is at 9:00 am, about when is the next one?" (answer in the speaker notes: about 9:25 pm)
