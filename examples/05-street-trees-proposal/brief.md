## The student's abstract

> **Cooling the street: do street trees make footpaths more comfortable on hot afternoons?**
>
> On summer afternoons, pedestrians in dense inner-city neighbourhoods walk on footpaths that absorb and re-radiate heat. Street trees are often promoted as a cooling measure, but councils need evidence at the scale of a single footpath segment, where people actually walk. This project will compare paired footpath segments, one under a continuous tree canopy and one without, on the same streets. On six clear summer afternoons between 2 pm and 4 pm, I will record surface temperature with a handheld infrared thermometer, and air temperature and humidity with a shaded data logger at 1.5 m height, at fixed points along each segment. I will also note surface material, sky view and wind conditions. Differences between shaded and unshaded segments will be compared with paired statistical tests. The study is limited to a small number of streets in one city and one season, and handheld measurements are sensitive to timing and sensor placement, so the findings will be presented as indicative rather than general. The expected contribution is a simple, repeatable footpath-scale measurement protocol that councils and community groups can reuse.

**Planned timeline (planning only):** site selection in November, measurements December–February, analysis and write-up March–May.
